ReCreate · Concrete strength assessment

In-situ concrete compressive strength from non-destructive and core tests

Estimate the compressive strength of concrete in existing structures and reclaimed precast elements from ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV), rebound hammer (RH) and SonReb results, calibrated with drilled cores. All strengths are normalised to a 150 × 300 mm reference cylinder.

Individual prediction

One NDT result in, one strength estimate out, from the ensemble of ten machine-learning models.

Test region: DT + NDT

Calibrate NDT readings against paired cores and extend them across a test region, following EN 13791:2019.

Test region: cores only

Characteristic strength and variability of a test region from destructive core tests alone.

Results are indicative statistics to support an engineer's assessment, not design values. The app is hosted on a free server, so the first request after a period of inactivity can take 2–3 minutes.

From the field

The prediction models

Each NDT method has its own CatBoost regression model, tuned with a Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) and trained with 10-fold cross-validation. Every prediction is the mean of the ten fold models. The models were trained on open databases of 16,531 paired NDT and compression results from 115 published studies, and use up to 17 (UPV), 15 (RH) or 22 (SonReb) input variables describing the concrete, the specimen and the test.

Test region analysis

A test region is a volume of concrete assumed to share the same mix and quality, such as a batch of similar reclaimed elements. In-situ strength is treated as lognormal, and the app returns the raw statistics needed to evaluate it: the characteristic strength fck,is, the coefficient of variation Vfc,is, its Student-t corrected value Vfc,is,corr, the bias factor and the sample-size coefficients.

DT + NDT

  • Calibration set (n ≥ 8): NDT readings paired with cores taken at the same locations.
  • Additional set (m ≥ 3): NDT readings elsewhere in the region, converted to strengths through the calibration.
  • Local calibration: a log-log regression fitted to your cores, following EN 13791:2019.
  • Global calibration: the machine-learning model gives the shape of the relationship and your cores set its level (a log-residual shift).
  • Both results are shown side by side, with plots against your cores and the global database.

Cores only

  • At least 8 cores, entered with their diameter, height and raw strength.
  • The coefficient of variation is taken as no less than 8%.
  • The same cores also set a lower bound on the variability in the DT + NDT analysis, so a calibration can never report less scatter than the cores themselves.

Core strengths are converted to the 150 × 300 mm cylinder using the same factors as the training databases, based on nominal core diameter and height-to-diameter ratio. The converted strength of each core is listed with the results.

Getting reliable results

Fill in optional fields where known. The models handle missing inputs, but every extra detail (age, aggregate size, design strength) improves the prediction.

Match the database spelling for device brands and standards, for example “PUNDIT” or “Original Schmidt Type N”. An unfamiliar spelling is treated as an unknown device.

Stay within the training ranges in the table above. The test-region results warn you when the mean core strength falls outside them.

Paste straight from Excel into the tables. Incomplete rows are flagged before the analysis runs.

About ReCreate

ReCreate (Reusing precast concrete for a circular economy) was an EU Horizon 2020 research project on the direct structural reuse of precast concrete elements in new buildings. It covered the full value chain, from deconstruction and quality control to logistics, redesign and reassembly, through an academic and industry consortium from Sweden, Finland, Germany, Croatia and the Netherlands, and pilot deconstruction projects in four of these countries. The project officially ended on 30 September 2026.

This app was developed in Work Package 4 (quality management) to reduce uncertainty about material properties before elements are reused. More at recreate-project.eu.
